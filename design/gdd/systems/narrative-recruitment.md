# System GDD — Narrative & Recruitment

## Intent

Tell a fresh mythic adventure in clear English while preserving simulation truth.

## Act grammar

Each turn is authored from typed facts:

1. **Cause:** what changed because of the previous result.
2. **Immediate problem:** a person, creature, place, and concrete threat.
3. **Choice:** two or three distinct actions with forecastable stakes.
4. **Resolution:** the simulation or rule result.
5. **Consequence:** one fact carried into a later turn.

The scene may be vivid, but it cannot add a second unrelated mystery, unnamed institution, or invented rule.

## Prose rules

- Use concrete subject–verb–object sentences and ordinary game terms.
- One invented proper noun per paragraph is the normal maximum.
- Prefer “The lion spirit shields the front row” to metaphor that obscures the action.
- Technique descriptions state effect, trigger, and cost in that order.
- Character descriptions use: origin → defining act → present desire → flaw → Awakening manifestation.
- Do not imitate or reproduce prose from existing fantasy works. Use them only as high-level genre references.

## Recruitment invariants

- Campaign creation presents three complete candidates but masks tank/damage/support labels.
- Only the selected hero exists in the active party at Turn 1.
- Candidate names, lore, skills, and future recruitment are not announced in the opening preamble.
- Companion one is introduced and recruited during Turn 2; companion two during Turn 3.
- A hero may appear in story or combat only after their introduction fact is true.
- Recruitment scenes establish why the hero joins, what they can do, and one unresolved personal desire.

## Relationship contract

Relationships store specific shared facts, not generic bond prose. Thresholds unlock a battle reaction, recovery benefit, scene option, or Awakening condition. A bond number without an enabled consequence is not player-facing.

## Choice and consequence

At least one Turn 1–3 choice must alter Turn 5 through enemy composition, terrain, reward, ally condition, or available option. The next scene repeats the authoritative result in the same meaning. Narrative never converts a combat defeat into a victory.

## Validation gates

- No unrecruited hero IDs in rendered cast, prose, formation, forecast, or battle.
- Every pronoun resolves to a present named subject.
- Every generated scene passes world vocabulary, required-fact, forbidden-fact, and length checks.
- A six-turn transcript reads coherently without inspecting engine state.
